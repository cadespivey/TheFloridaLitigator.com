Introduction  
Rule 1.010 of the Florida Rules of Civil Procedure and the Rules of Judicial 
Administration encourages the speedy, just and inexpensive determination of every action, and 
imposes on the trial Court the duty to monitor and manage the docket in order to achieve this 
goal. These policies and procedures are intended to efficiently and effectively apply the Florida 
Rules of Civil Procedure, which shall control if there is any conflict between the two. 
Communications with the Judicial Office 
1. Method of Communication: All communications to the judicial office must be 
submitted by e-mail to polkl@circuit8.org. The subject line must contain the case number, case 
name, and relevant matter (e.g., 2024-CA-000005 – Doe v. Doe - 2-Hour Hearing Requested.). 
2. Ex parte Communications: All communications with the judicial office must comply 
with Canon 3 of the Code of Judicial Conduct, which prohibits a judge from initiating,

permitting, or considering ex parte communications and from considering other communications 
outside the presence of the parties concerning a pending or impending proceeding, unless

3. Unsolicited Communications: Unsolicited communications from non-parties will not 
be considered by the court. Parties may only contact the judicial office in accordance with these 
practices and procedures. 
4. E-Filing Portal Contact Information: All attorneys and self-represented litigants must 
provide an e-mail address to receive signed orders electronically, unless excused. Fla. R. Gen. 
Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.516. It is the responsibility of attorneys and self-represented litigants to 
update their contact information using Form 2.603 any time there is a change in the e-mail 
account registered for electronic service. 
5. Response to Inquiries: The judicial assistant is not authorized to provide legal advice.  
If the judicial assistant is out of the office, the judicial assistant’s out-of-office procedures will be 
included in the judicial assistant’s automatic response. 
Scheduling Procedures 
1. Scheduling Hearings: The parties are to contact the judicial assistant by email at 
polkl@circuit8.org to request a hearing on any matter.  The hearing request must include a copy 
of the motion that you are requesting a hearing on and the amount of time requested for the 
hearing.   
2. Notice of Hearing: Once a hearing has been confirmed with the Court, the parties are 
to file the notice of hearing and email a copy of the notice to the judicial assistant. 
3. Submission Deadlines: All hearing materials must be sent and received by the judicial 
assistant no later than 7 business days prior to the hearing in hard copy along with any case law 
or memorandums of law.  
4. Continuance Procedure: The court will not consider any motion to continue without a 
client consent and good faith effort statement. 
5. Cancelling Hearings: If the parties resolve a motion prior to the hearing, or any 
portion of the motion, Counsel shall immediately advise the Court of the resolution.

Motion Practice and Scheduling Hearings  
1. Administrative Order 3.09, appendix A Standing Case Management Order effective 
4/30/21 requires that before filing a motion, the moving party shall confer with counsel for the 
opposing party in a good faith effort to resolve the issues raised by the motion.  The moving 
party shall file with the motion, a statement certifying that the moving counsel has conferred 
with opposing counsel and stating whether counsel agree on the resolution of the motion. A 
certification to the effect that opposing counsel was unavailable for a conference before filing a 
motion is insufficient to satisfy the parties’ obligation to confer. The moving party retains the 
duty to contact opposing counsel expeditiously after filing and to supplement the motion 
promptly with a statement certifying whether or to what extent the parties have resolved the 
issue(s) presented in the motion. Counsel for the non-moving party is obligated to promptly reply 
to a ‘meet-and-confer’ request and to provide availability for such conference. If the interested 
parties agree to all or part of the relief sought in any motion, the caption of the motion shall 
include the word “unopposed,” “agreed,” or “stipulated” or otherwise succinctly inform the 
reader that, as to all or part of the requested relief, no opposition exists.  
All motions do not require a hearing, and the Court may rule on motions without a 
hearing and without prior notice to counsel. 
Setting Matters for Trial (Jury and Non-Jury)  
When a case is at issue, any party may file a Motion to Set Matter for Trial.  The parties 
may obtain a trial date without the requirement to appear for a hearing by filing a motion to set 
trial setting forth the agreed trial date and term of the trial and providing it by email to 
polkl@circuit8.org. The Court’s available trial weeks are on the Court’s web page.  If counsel 
seek a trial date outside of the Fla. Jud. Time Standards, an explanation as to why it needs to be 
set beyond the time standards and a detailed discovery plan must be provided. 
IF THE CASE SETTLES AFTER IT IS SET FOR TRIAL, THE PARTIES SHALL 
IMMEDIATELY NOTIFY THE COURT SO THAT THE TRIAL AND ALL PENDING 
HEARINGS MAY BE REMOVED FROM THE COURT’S CALENDAR.

Proposed Orders Following a Hearing  
The Court may request the lawyers prepare proposed orders pursuant to the Court’s verbal 
recitations of rulings made during a hearing. Such proposed orders after a hearing are to be 
timely submitted to the Court as follows:   
1. If a time is not set for submission of the proposed order, the order should be 
submitted within seven (7) days of the hearing.  
2. Proposed orders should be limited to ten pages unless leave of Court for a longer 
order is obtained. 
3. Proposed orders should not contain argument. 
4. If there is an unrepresented party involved in the case not using e-Portal, the proposed 
Order must be submitted to the Court in writing and emailed in word format, with sufficient

Further, the party presenting the proposed Order, shall be responsible for providing addressed,

6. The proposed Order service list must contain e-filing addresses for opposing

Courtesy Copies of Case Law and Other Documents  
1. Copies of motions and memoranda must be delivered to the Court at least seven 
business days before a scheduled hearing on the matter.  Motions, pleadings, memoranda, and 
case law may be provided by e-mail as well as U.S. Mail or hand delivery. However, voluminous 
pleadings (more than 10 pages, including attachments) must be provided via hand-delivery, U.S. 
Mail, or other delivery service. Any binders containing case law shall be received by the Court 
at least 7 business days prior to the hearing. 
2. The Court requires highlighted copies of all pertinent case law to be provided in hard 
copy. It is not necessary to provide copies of case law to the standards of review for run of the 
mill motions such as motion for summary judgment, motion to dismiss, and discovery motions. 
3. The Court may cancel the hearing if the required materials are not received. 
4. Case law emailed to the Court less than 7 days prior to the hearing will not be 
considered. 
 
The Court encourages all parties to work together where possible to submit one copy of 
the supporting materials necessary for the Court’s consideration.

Pretrial Procedures and Case Management Conferences 
1. The case management conference order requires specific information needed by the 
Court to schedule the trial.  Please ensure that all required information is included. 
2. The case management statement must be filed no later than 15 working days prior to 
the case management conference. 
3. The parties must confer to determine if they can agree to a trial date, the length of the 
trial and advise the Court of same at least 5 days prior to the case management conference.  
4. Please ensure that you confirm your client’s availability for trial suggestion prior to 
the submission of a case management statement and/or appearance at the case management 
conference. 
Other Division Procedures: Motions for Rehearing and Motions for New Trial  
These motions should first be e-filed with the Clerk of Court. The party filing the motion 
shall provide the Court with a copy for review by U.S. Mail, hand delivery or email at the time 
the motion is filed. Any motion over 20 pages should not be emailed but delivered by U.S. Mail 
or hand delivery. If a hearing is required, the Court will schedule a hearing with the parties.   
 
Discovery Disputes  
As with all motions filed in this Division (as set forth above), prior to filing a motion to 
compel discovery, the moving party shall confer in good faith with the non-moving party in an 
attempt to resolve the discovery dispute. If, for any reason, the moving party is unable to confer 
with counsel for the non-moving party, the motion should describe at least 3 attempts to confer 
by the moving party.

Motions to Continue 
1. The Court will review Motions to Continue to determine good cause and rule upon 
the motion without a hearing.  The failure to timely pursue discovery, take depositions, procure 
experts, and conduct mediation do not constitute good cause and any motion to continue alleging 
same will be denied without hearing.  
2. The motion to continue must be accompanied by the Client’s consent. 
3. If a party represents that a motion to continue is unopposed, then all parties’ consents 
to the continuance must be filed. 
4. If the parties jointly agree to submit a motion to the Court without the need for a 
hearing, the moving party should forward to the Court the motion and a proposed agreed order.   
5. Hearings will only be set on motions already filed with the Clerk which contain the 
required certification of good faith conference. All hearings must be coordinated with opposing 
counsel. The party requesting a hearing on any pending motion should contact the Judicial 
Assistant with all other parties on the line or by email at polkl@circuit8.org. Dates provided for 
hearings are not held and may be given to other cases. All counsel shall promptly respond to 
the moving party’s request with proposed dates received from the Judicial Assistant to facilitate 
this process. Hearing dates/times are not secured until confirmation is sent from the Court.  
Hearing times not timely noticed will be removed from the Court’s calendar.  
6. Once a hearing time is set, no party may add or notice additional matters for that time 
without the express consent of all parties and the Court.  
7. The parties shall not include the judicial assistant in any discussions about any 
substantive matter or disputes about whether a motion is appropriate. Emails to the judicial 
assistant should be limited to dates, times and amount of time needed for the hearing. 
Unprofessional communications to the judicial assistant will result in a referral to the local bench 
bar committee.  
8. 
The Court’s hearing schedule has very little, and sometimes no, time between 
hearings. Please arrive early and allow plenty of time for parking if appearing in person or for 
connecting to the hearing if appearing remotely. Failure to timely appear for a hearing may result 
in the motion being denied (if the moving party) or the motion being heard without you (if the 
non-moving party). Moreover, the Court will enforce the time limits set for each hearing. Any

ADA Accommodations and Interpreter Requests: 
1.  Eighth Judicial Circuit of Florida provides ADA accommodations, such as sign 
language interpreters, assistive listening devices, and document formatting (Braille/large print), 
to ensure individuals with disabilities have access to court programs and services. Requests 
should be submitted using the ADA Request Form preferably seven days in advance.  
2. Request Process: Complete the ADA Accommodation Request Form and submit it 
to the local ADA coordinator as far in advance as possible. 
3. Available Services: Assistive listening devices, qualified interpreters (ASL or other), 
CART (real-time transcription), and qualified readers. 
4. Limitations: The court cannot provide transportation to the courthouse, personal 
devices (wheelchairs, hearing aids), legal counsel, or official transcripts as an accommodation. 
5. Procedure Requests: Requests involving case procedures, such as rescheduling, 
telephonic appearances, or remote video participation, must be made by written motion to the 
presiding judge, not via the ADA request form.  
6. For more information, you can visit the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) 
Information page for the Eighth Judicial Circuit of Florida.
