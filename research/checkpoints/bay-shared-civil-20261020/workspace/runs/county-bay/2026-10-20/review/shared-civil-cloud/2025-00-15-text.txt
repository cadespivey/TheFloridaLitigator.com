RE: COURTHOUSE SECURITY 
 
WHEREAS increased threats and acts of violence directed at witnesses, jurors, litigants, 
lawyers, court personnel, and judges throughout our nation and locally demonstrate the need for 
additional security at our county courthouses, courthouse annexes, and any “off-site” courtrooms 
or office buildings in the Fourteenth Judicial Circuit. 
 
WHEREAS, the protection of the public, the jurors, and courthouse personnel is of the 
utmost concern of the judges of the Fourteenth Judicial Circuit, and by such, a major component 
of a prompt and efficient administration of justice is preserved. 
 
WHEREAS, pursuant to Article V, Section 2(d) of the Florida Constitution, section 43.26, 
Florida Statutes, and Rule 2.215(b)(2) and (7), Florida Rules of General Practice and Judicial 
Administration, establishes that the chief judge of a circuit shall exercise administrative 
supervision over all courts within the judicial circuit, and “shall regulate the use of all court 
facilities”; and 
 
WHEREAS Rule 2.215(b)(3) and (6), Florida Rules of General Practice and Judicial 
Administration, directs the chief judge to develop an administrative plan for the efficient and 
proper administration of all courts within the circuit and accordingly finds it necessary and 
appropriate to establish responsibilities and guidelines related to courthouse security; and 
 
WHEREAS, section 29.008(1)(e), Florida Statutes, requires counties to fund the cost of 
security of facilities for the circuit and county courts including the security needs of such buildings 
and other court related functions. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, it is ORDERED and ADJUDGED that the following procedures 
concerning courthouse security shall be followed: 
 
I. 
General Provisions 
 
ALL PERSONS, with the exception of those specifically enumerated in Section II of this 
Order, shall be prohibited from carrying or possessing firearms, antique or replica firearms, 
weapons, or destructive devices, as defined in section 790.001, Florida Statutes, in a courthouse,

courthouse annex, “off-site” courthouse office building or courtroom (hereinafter collectively 
referred to as the “courthouse”) in the six counties of the Fourteenth Judicial Circuit. Further, ALL 
PERSONS are prohibited from carrying or being in possession of any other device(s) or 
instrument(s) that could be used or perceived to be used as a physical threat to persons or property 
in the courthouses of the Fourteenth Judicial Circuit. 
 
Prohibitions may include, but are not limited to, the following: 
 
• 
Pocket knives of any size. 
• 
Scissors. 
• 
Firearms, stun guns, long guns, or any item which shoots a projectile. 
• 
Ammunition. 
• 
Fireworks. 
• 
Flammable liquids. 
 
To enforce the prohibition, each Sheriff and his/her appointed deputies or bailiff are 
empowered to stop all persons entering the courthouses to conduct a security search. Security 
equipment, purchased by funds provided by the county, which will allow for the scanning of person 
and of property in the least intrusive means practicable should be used to assist the deputies and 
bailiffs to determine the person’s compliance with the administrative order and ensure the safety 
of the occupants of the courthouse. The discovery of illegal weapons, contraband as defined in 
Section 932.701, Florida Statutes, or other violations of criminal statutes will result in the 
immediate notification of law enforcement so that an arrest and seizure can be made. 
 
The prohibition of carrying weapons into a court facility supersedes any local law, 
ordinance or local rule that may otherwise authorize possession of a weapon or firearm in a county-
owned facility. 
 
Persons refusing to submit to a search of their person and effects shall be denied access 
into the courthouse. 
 
II. 
Exempt Persons 
 
The following individuals are exempt from the prohibition against carrying FIREARMS 
into the courthouse, based on their job, position, and duties. However, such persons SHALL NOT 
carry firearms into the courthouse if the purpose of their visit to the courthouse is personal. 
 
• 
All certified law enforcement officers, including federal, state, county, and city, while 
on official law enforcement business. 
• 
Active and Senior Judges; and 
• 
Magistrates and Hearing Officers licensed to carry a concealed weapon of firearm 
pursuant to section 790.06, Florida Statutes; and 
• 
Clerks and Evidence Custodians according to their policies; and 
• 
Contracted Armored Car Personnel; and 
• 
Marshalls and Judges from the Florida Supreme Court and First District Court of 
Appeal; and

Exceptional or Emergency Circumstances 
At their discretion, and Sheriffs of the 14th Judicial Circuit may temporarily prohibit any 
persons authorized by this administrative order from carrying weapons into a court facility. 
 
III. 
Admission Searches 
 
A. 
Any person (excluding courthouse personnel entering the secured personnel 
entrance) desiring to enter the courthouse shall enter through the authorized entrance 
and pass through security equipment (e.g., magnetometer) where available or scanned 
by a hand-held scanning device. 
 
B. 
Any person who activates the security equipment will be asked to empty his/her 
pockets and to pass through the security device a second time. If, after this second 
review, security concerns remain, the person shall be subject to a more thorough 
search to identify all objects which activated the security equipment. 
 
C. 
Any person, prior to passing through any security equipment, shall place any purse, 
package, briefcase, wallet, computer or other electronic equipment, or any other item 
into the container provided and allow the equipment to either be physically inspected 
by the security officer or scanned by the security equipment. 
 
D. 
Any person with a pacemaker, internal cardiac defibrillator, or other such device 
should notify the security officer and present a medical card confirming such so that 
the person can avoid the magnetometer (if being used). The person will then be 
subject to security checks which may include use of a hand-held device. 
 
E. 
If illegal firearms, weapons, destructive devices, or contraband as defined above are 
found during a search, an arrest and seizure may occur. 
 
F. 
All courthouse personnel shall either enter through the separate secured personnel 
entrance (if applicable) or, if they choose to enter through the main entrance, must 
show courthouse ID badge bearing their photo, to avoid security screening.

IV. 
Notice 
 
One of the following two notices shall be posted in a conspicuous place at all courthouse 
entrances where applicable: 
 
FOR THE PROTECTION OF THE PUBLIC 
 
All Persons and Personal Belongings Entering the Courthouse Will be Searched. No 
Weapons of Any Kind or Any Item that can be used as a Firearm or Weapon is 
Allowed on this Property Except by Sworn Law Enforcement Officers on Official 
Duty. Please be Patient. The Security Checkpoint is for YOUR Safety. Thank you. 
 
-- or -- 
 
For the Protection of the Public, All Persons Entering these Premises Must Pass 
Through the Magnetometer and May be Subject to a Limited Search. All Packages, 
Briefcases, Pocketbooks, or Other Items Carried by a Person Must be Offered for 
Inspection if Such Person Wishes to Enter the Building. Illegal Weapons, firearms of 
any kind, or other Contraband Discovered in the Course of These Search Procedures 
Will be Seized and the Person Found in Possession of Such Items May be Arrested 
and Apprehended on Site. 
 
V. 
For All Courthouses  
 
All attorneys, who are members in good standing of the Florida Bar, desiring to enter a 
Courthouse and avoid security screening, may show picture ID and the attorney’s Florida 
Bar Card and/or current 14th Circuit attorney card.  Persons exempt from this requirement 
include all elected officials and constitutional officers. 
 
 
DONE AND ORDERED in Chambers, at Panama City, Bay County, this 8th day of
