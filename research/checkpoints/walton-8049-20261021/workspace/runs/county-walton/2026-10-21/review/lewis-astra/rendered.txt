A. Communications with the Judicial Office

Method of Communication: All communications to the judicial office must be submitted by email to anna.lee@flcourts1.gov with copy to all opposing counsel and each self-represented litigant. The subject line of any email must contain the case number and case name (e.g., 2024 CA 001234 – Doe v. Doe).
Ex parte Communications Prohibited: All communications with the judicial office must comply with Canon 3 of the Code of Judicial Conduct, which prohibits a judge from initiating, permitting, or considering ex parte communications and other communications outside the presence of the parties concerning a pending or impending proceeding, unless authorized by law. All parties must be copied on any email directed to the judicial office, unless authorized by law.
Unsolicited Communications: Parties may only contact the judicial office in accordance with these practices and procedures. Unsolicited communications from non-parties will not be considered by the court.
E-Filing Portal Contact Information: All attorneys and self-represented litigants must provide an email address to receive signed orders electronically. Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.516. It is the responsibility of attorneys and for all self-represented litigants to update their contact information using Form 2.603 anytime there is a change in the email address that has been registered for electronic service. When an attorney is permitted to withdraw leaving a party unrepresented, the attorney shall assist the former client with instructions on how to register their email address and provide a link to any unrepresented to these directions and operating procedures. The withdrawing attorney shall also warn their former client that failure to register their email address and keep it current may result in them not receiving court filings in their case.
Response to Inquiries: The judicial assistant is not authorized to provide legal advice. When the judicial assistant is out of the office, you will receive an out-of-office reply with a projected return date.
Other Communication Procedures: Email shall be utilized for scheduling hearings. All matters to be considered by the Court should be in the form of a motion or pleading, filed and served on all parties. Attempts to argue substantive issues through email will not be tolerated. Moreover, the judicial assistant shall not be included in conferrals between the parties regarding availability or otherwise discussing the case.
B. Scheduling Procedures
Scheduling Hearings: In addition to complying with the terms of Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.202, the Court strongly encourages the parties to meet and confer when possible. A hearing will not be set without the required certificate. Setting a hearing signals to the Court that there is an objection to the motion or the moving party has not been able to confer despite good faith attempts to do so.
MEET AND CONFER: The term "confer" requires a substantive conversation, in person or by telephone, in a good faith effort to resolve the motion without court action and does not envision a written exchange of ultimatums. Counsel who merely attempt to confer have not conferred. Counsel shall respond promptly to inquiries and communications from opposing counsel. As to discovery dispute motions in particular, the Court may sua sponte deny motions that fail to include an appropriate and complete Certificate of Compliance.
All hearings must be scheduled by email, please note the following:
Dates will not be given until the filed motion is “showing” in the case.
Opposing counsel and any self-represented litigant must be included in the email.
Your email must include the following:
Case Number
Party Names
Type of Hearing (Motion(s) to be set) (Include CCIS document number and date filed)
Length requested for hearing (make certain you request enough time for both sides to be heard)
Please notate in your email if this hearing is time sensitive due to an upcoming trial date.
Please do not make repetitive requests. Emails are answered in the order they are received as time allows.
After obtaining available dates, coordinate with opposing counsel to select dates that work for all parties. Once the parties have agreed on the potential date(s), please inform the judicial assistant via email, and she will reply to confirm that the time slot is still available, and the hearing is being set. The hearing is not considered set until you receive confirmation from the judicial assistant.
Dates will not be protected/final until the judge’s office is notified that the hearing date has been coordinated.
Hearings may be held in-person or via Zoom teleconference. A motion and proposed order must be e-filed for requests to appear via Zoom for hearings over one hour. All parties must agree to appear via Zoom, no hybrid hearings will be allowed.
Notice of Hearing: The Notice of Hearing must be filed and served immediately after reserving hearing.
Notice of Hearings must include the date, time, type of hearing, document name and sequence number (e.g., DN 367,etc.) and location (in-person at the courthouse or Zoom teleconference). As a reminder, Walton County is located in the Central Time Zone.
Hearing notices must contain the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) notification required by Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.540.
No additional motions should be added to the existing hearing time without first coordinating with the judicial assistant and other counsel/parties. The Court will not hear motions that have not been properly scheduled and noticed. 
Submission Deadlines: Any memoranda and/or case law that parties wish for the judge to consider prior to a hearing should be submitted in binders at least three business days before the hearing
Order of Proceedings: The amount of time for the hearing is typically allocated equally among the party. The moving party will argue first, then the opposing party, and then the moving party will be allowed rebuttal argument if time remains.
Continuance Procedure: Motions for continuance must state with specificity: (1) the basis of the need for the continuance, including when the basis became known to the movant and (2) whether the motion is opposed. Except for good cause shown, the motion must be signed by the party requesting the continuance, as required by Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.545(e).
Cancelling Hearings:
You must cancel hearings by notifying the judicial assistant immediately via email with a courtesy copy of the Notice of Cancellation attached. Additionally, in the event the cancellation is due to a complete settlement of all remaining issues, a proposed, stipulated order of dismissal in a form agreed to by the parties should be submitted not more than thirty days after settlement is reached. The parties must advise the court if additional time is required to conclude the settlement term. The Notice of Cancellation must be served upon opposing counsel and any self-represented litigant. If the hearing is scheduled to occur within 24 hours, a phone call to the judicial assistant at 850-892-5425 is also required.
The ONLY way the judge knows of a cancellation is through your notification. If the hearing is cancelled far enough in advance, the time can be made available to others, so your adherence is appreciated.

CROSS-SETTING OR “PIGGY-BACKING” HEARINGS

When a hearing is initially scheduled, the time set aside for the original motion is deemed to be sufficient for that matter only. Adding additional motions is not possible unless there are no objections by opposing counsel/parties to doing so and adequate additional time is available. No added motions will be heard that are not filed at least 5 days before the hearing. All objections and/or responses should be filed no later than two days before the hearing.
Scheduling Procedures for Motions for Summary Judgment
In keeping with the Requirements of Fla.R.Civ.P. 1.510, a party asserting that a fact cannot be, or is generally disputed, must support the assertion by :
Citing to particular parts of the materials in the record OR
Showing that the materials cited do not establish the absence or presence of genuine dispute, or that an adverse party cannot produce admissible evidence to support the fact.
At the time of filing the motion for summary judgment, the moving party must file its supporting factual position s provided in subdivision (1) of the rule.
The non-moving party has forty (40) days after service of the motion for summary judgment in which to file a response that includes the non-moving party’s supporting factual position as provided in subdivision (1) in the rule.
No hearing on a motion for summary judgment shall be heard unless set for hearing a date which is not less than 10 days after the deadline for filing a response to the motion for summary judgment.
The court expects the moving party to fully identify and cite all parts of the record that support granting the motion. Each motion should include a memorandum of law explaining the elements of the cause of action for which summary judgment is being sought along with a memo of law pertaining to the basis for the court granting the motion. At minimum, citations to controlling opinions supporting the motion should be included pending. This should be filed with the motion by the moving party. Likewise, any filing in opposition to a motion for summary judgment should contain citations to all portions of the record which the non-moving party asserts support the existence of a genuine issue of material fact so as to preclude granting of the motion for summary judgment. All filings in support or in opposition to a motion for summary judgment must be filed not less than ten (10) days prior to a notice hearing on the motion.
Note that except for good cause shown, the court will not set a motion for summary judgment for a hearing of more than 30 minutes in duration (15 minutes for each side to argue).A motion and filing in opposition with proper citations in the record along with a memorandum of law should streamline a determination of all such motions.

Requirements and Etiquette for Zoom or Remote Video Hearings:
Dress code and behavior are the same on Zoom as if you were appearing in the courtroom. This includes consumption of food or beverages during the hearing.
Those appearing via Zoom should ensure that they are familiar with the platform and shall sign in early to ensure timely appearance. As some hearings are set for as little as ten minutes, failure to appear on time may result in a waiver of objection(s) or other sanctions.
Please ensure that your username reflects your full name and case number in lieu of a nickname or screenname.
A private location that is quiet and free from distractions is required. Under no circumstances will a participant be permitted to appear remotely while driving.
Your camera must be active when joining the proceeding and kept on until instructed otherwise by the court.
Your microphone must be muted until instructed otherwise by the court.
If a witness appears remotely, the party calling the witness must ensure the witness has a functioning camera and microphone and has tested the internet connection before the hearing. The oath will be administered in accordance with the Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.530.

D. Submission of Orders and Judgments
Format: All proposed orders must be submitted in Microsoft Word format through the e-filing system unless otherwise instructed.
Submission Method:
All proposed orders should be e-filed through the proposed order portion of the Florida Courts E-Filing Portal with a cover letter under separate cover advising that all parties agree to the contents/language of the proposed order. You may contact the judicial assistant at (850) 892-5425 if you need assistance.
All proposed orders relating to a hearing that counsel are NOT in agreement should be emailed to the judicial assistant at anna.lee@flcourts1.gov in Microsoft Word format. The proposed order must be accompanied by a cover letter with a statement identifying any disagreement of the parties as to the proposed order. The proposed orders must include the names and email addresses of all parties who will need to receive e-service of same. The use of “copies too all counsel of record” is strongly discouraged.
Deadline for Submissions: Propose orders must be submitted within the time provided by the Court in a separate order or verbally at the hearing. In no event should the time for submission exceed thirty (30) days.

Note: Please do not send proposed orders to the Judge's office prior to a hearing. This office does not have the resources necessary to be responsible for receiving, holding and retrieving the proposed orders for a pending hearing. It is preferred that proposed orders be submitted as described above.

E. Courtesy Copies of Case Law and Other Documents:
When Required: Courtesy copies of case law and exhibits must be submitted to the court for any evidentiary proceeding within a reasonable period of time before such hearing. This Court is not equipped to print large volume exhibits intended to be introduced into evidence. A thumb drive with all documents should be clearly labelled to coincide with their content. e.g., Exhibit 1 – Marriage License. Any thumb drive provided to the court should be free of any documents and exhibits other than are to be submitted for consideration by the court. Please make sure that only “clean” thumb drives are used to provide these documents to the court. Counsel should check with the Clerk of Court for Walton County for directions on filing and use of exhibits at hearings or trial.
Deadline for Submissions: Courtesy copies must be submitted to the court no later than three (3) business days before any hearing or trial. Unless otherwise specified by the court.
Other Courtesy Copies Procedures: N/A

F. Emergency and Other Urgent Matters
Requirements: Other than as provided in Fla.R.Civ.P. 1.160, if a party believes there is a factual basis for setting an emergency hearing, a detailed motion setting forth the following must be filed: (1) the issues to be resolved, (2) reasons why an emergency hearing is necessary, and (3) the amount of time needed for each party’s presentation.
Scheduling: If the court determines that an emergency exists, a hearing may be scheduled unilaterally by the court, but an attempt will be made to accommodate schedules. All parties shall make themselves available for the emergency hearing, barring exigent circumstances.
Other Procedures Relating to Emergency and Other Urgent Matters: N/A

G. Exhibits for Evidentiary Proceedings
Submission Method: For voluminous exhibits, binders are strongly encouraged and should be submitted via hand delivery or mail to the judge’s office. All attorneys and self-represented litigants must bring sufficient copies of each exhibit for the clerk, the Court, and each party to review during the hearing or trial. Notebooks should be no more than 300 pages per notebook (documents can be two sided). D ring notebooks are preferred but not required.
Format: Exhibits which are voluminous may be submitted in a thumb drive which contain all documents and exhibits in PDF format. A thumb drive with all documents should be clearly labelled to coincide with their content. e.g., “Exhibit 1 – Marriage License”, Exhibit 2 – Plaintiff’s Hospital Bill HCA Anytown, etc. If submitting via thumb drive to Judge Lewis, it should be clearly marked for Judge Lewis and will not be submitted to the clerk or returned after the hearing. Any thumb drive shall be free of any malware and shall contain only documents and exhibits relevant to the hearing at which they are being presented.
Deadline for Submissions (See also “Courtesy Copies of Case Law and Other Documents” above): Any memorandum and/or case law that parties wish for the judge to consider prior to a hearing should be emailed or e-filed at least three business days before the hearing. If said documents are voluminous, please submit binders three business days before the hearing.
Other Procedures Relating to Exhibits for Evidentiary Proceedings: N/A
H. Pretrial Procedures and Conferences
Case Management Conference: Case Management Conferences (CMCs) are required to get a firm trial date. When requesting dates for a Case Management Conference, please advise if it is to schedule a trial date and if the trial is jury or non-jury. Non-jury trials over three hours must schedule a CMC to set the trial.
Requirements: If no objection is filed to the Notice of Readiness for Trial, the moving party must schedule the CMC or status conference through the hearing instructions below.
Scheduling: Contact the judicial assistant via email at anna.lee@flcourts1.gov to coordinate a CMC date.
I. Setting Case for Trial
Procedure: A party will file a Notice for Trial or Motion for Case Management Conference. Thereafter, a Case Management Conference will be set by coordination of the attorneys.
Notice Period: A pretrial date will be scheduled at the time of setting a trial date to be included in the Case Management Order following the hearing.
Trial counsel and parties are expected to appear in person at pretrial conferences unless otherwise advised.
Other Procedures for Setting Case for Trial: Mediation is required unless otherwise waived by the Court by motion and order. Remediation may be required if a case has been mediated more than one year prior to trial.
