I. 
Communications with the Judicial Office 
 
A. 
Method of Communication 
 
1. 
All communications to the judicial office must be submitted by e-
mail to judicial assistant at williamsa@jud14.flcourts.org. The 
subject line of any e-mail to the judicial assistant must contain 
the case name and case number (e.g., Doe v. Doe, 25CA1234). 
 
2. 
Self-represented litigants and attorneys excused from e-mail 
service may communicate with the judicial office by telephone 
call to 850-638-6255. The judicial office does not accept text 
messages. 
 
B. 
Ex Parte Communication 
 
1. 
All communications with the judicial office must comply with 
Canon 3 of the Code of Judicial Conduct, which prohibits a judge 
from 
initiating, 
permitting, 
or 
considering 
ex 
parte 
communications and from considering other communications 
outside the presence of the parties concerning a pending or 
impending proceeding, unless authorized by law. All parties must 
be copied on any e-mail directed to the judicial office, unless an 
ex parte communication is authorized by law. 
C. 
Unsolicited Communication 
 
1. 
Parties may only contact the judicial office in accordance with 
these practices and procedures. Unsolicited communications 
from non-parties will not be considered by the court.  
D. 
E-Filing Portal Contact Information 
 
1. 
All attorneys and self-represented litigants must make and 
receive service by e-mail, which is generally through the Florida 
Courts E-Filing Portal, unless excused. Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. 
Admin. 2.516. 
2. 
All attorneys and self-represented litigants must provide an e-
mail address to receive signed orders electronically, unless 
excused.  Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.516. It is the 
responsibility of attorneys and self-represented litigants to 
update their contact information through the Clerk’s office using 
Form 2.603 any time there is a change in the e-mail account 
registered for electronic service.

E. 
Response to Inquiries 
 
1. 
The judicial assistant is not authorized to provide legal advice. 
 
2. 
The judicial assistant strives to substantively respond to all 
inquiries within one business day. If the judicial assistant is 
unable to substantively respond within one business day, your 
message will be acknowledged as received with an indication of 
when to expect a response. 
 
3. 
When the judicial assistant is out of the office, your message will 
be acknowledged as received with an indication of when to expect 
a substantive response and an alternate contact for immediate 
assistance. 
 
F. 
Other Communication Procedures 
 
1. 
Any e-mail sent to or from the judicial office may be a public 
record subject to disclosure. 
 
II. 
Scheduling Procedures 
 
A. 
Court Schedule 
 
1. 
Trials are scheduled by the Court. 
 
B. 
Scheduling Hearing 
 
1. 
It is the responsibility of the attorney or pro se litigant who files 
any motion to ensure that it is timely scheduled on the Court's 
calendar and there is sufficient and adequate notice provided to 
opposing counsel and any other witnesses required for the 
hearing. Parties should anticipate a delay of at least twenty (20) 
days between the request for a hearing lasting longer than fifteen 
(15) minutes and the actual hearing date. Thus, counsel should 
request 
appropriate 
hearing 
time 
in 
advance 
of 
the 
aforementioned deadlines. 
 
2. 
Hearings must be requested by e-mail to the judicial assistant at 
williamsa@jud14.flcourts.org and copying opposing counsel and 
any self-represented litigant. Include the following in your 
request: 
 
a. 
Case style; 
b. 
Case number; 
c. 
Type of motion to be heard;

d. 
Amount of time needed; 
e. 
First date movant is available for hearing; and 
f. 
Names of counsel. 
 
3. 
The judicial assistant will “reply all” to the email with available 
dates, times, and instructions. Be aware that Washington County 
is in the central time zone. 
 
4. 
Do not include the judicial assistant in e-mail chains between 
counsel/staff relative to dates when each, or the other, are 
available. 
 
5. 
The judicial assistant will send an e-mail confirming the date and 
time of the hearing. Do not file notices of hearing before receiving 
the confirmation e-mail from the judicial assistant. 
 
6. 
Email the judicial assistant a copy of all notices for hearing once 
filed. 
 
7. 
The Court will generally schedule all hearings requiring more 
than one hour to be held in person. All other hearings may be 
scheduled via Zoom upon agreement of all parties. Contact the 
judicial assistant for Zoom information. Requests for an in-
person hearing must be made at the time of coordinating the 
hearing. 
 
8. 
Hearing dates will not be provided for motions that have not been 
filed in the case. 
 
9. 
Any motions noticed, but not specifically scheduled with, or 
confirmed by, the judicial assistant, will not be heard. 
 
10. 
Additional motions cannot be piggybacked on a previously 
scheduled hearing time without being cleared with the judicial 
assistant and opposing counsel, and provided sufficient time has 
been reserved. To add a motion to a previously scheduled 
hearing, obtain opposing counsel’s consent to the addition, then 
email the judicial assistant about adding the motion to the 
hearing. The judicial assistant will confirm the addition of the 
motion to the hearing time. Do not file a notice of the added 
motion until receipt of the judicial assistant’s confirmation.

F. 
Continuance Procedure 
 
1. 
Motions for continuance must be submitted at least  three (3) 
business days prior to the scheduled court date for which the 
continuance is sought, barring exigent circumstances. Except for 
good cause shown, the motion must be signed by the party 
requesting the continuance, as required by Florida Rule of 
General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.545(e). 

=== PAGE 6 ===
Page 6 of 12 
 
2. 
Motions for continuance are disfavored and will be granted only 
upon good cause shown. Successive continuances are highly 
disfavored. Lack of due diligence is not grounds for granting a 
continuance. Except for good cause shown, the motion must be 
signed by the party requesting the continuance, as required by 
Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 
2.545(e). 
 
3. 
Motions for continuance must state with specificity: (1) the basis 
of the need for the continuance, including when the basis became 
known to the movant; (2) whether the motion is opposed; (3) the 
action and specific dates for the action that will enable the 
movant to be ready, including, but not limited to, confirming the 
specific date any required participants are available; and (4) the 
proposed date by which the case will be ready to proceed and 
whether that date is agreed by all parties. Except for good cause 
shown, the motion must be signed by the party requesting the 
continuance, as required by Florida Rule of General Practice and 
Judicial Administration 2.545(e). 
 
G. 
Cancelling Hearings 
 
1. 
Notify the judicial assistant by e-mail of all hearing cancellations 
and trial settlements as promptly as possible. You must also 
immediately file and serve a notice of cancellation on opposing 
counsel and any self-represented litigant.  
 
2. 
Parties may not cancel a trial without first having filed a Notice 
of Voluntary Dismissal. An Order of Dismissal must be provided 
to the Court. 
 
III. 
Remote Appearance 
 
A. 
Remote Appearance Procedure 
 
1. 
The court maintains a hybrid virtual courtroom, allowing parties 
to appear either in person or remotely, as provided by Florida 
Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.530. 
 
2. 
Requests for an in-person hearing must be made at the time of 
coordinating the hearing. 
 
3. 
Any objection to the use of remote appearance must be filed no 
later than fourteen (14) days prior to the hearing.

C. 
Requirements 
 
1. 
Any person appearing remotely must be in a private location that 
is quiet and free from distractions. Under no circumstances will 
a participant be permitted to appear remotely from a moving 
vehicle. 
 
2. 
Any person appearing remotely must dress and behave 
professionally in the same manner as if physically present in the 
courtroom. 
 
3. 
Any person appearing remotely must enable the person’s camera 
when joining the proceeding and keep the camera turned on until 
instructed otherwise by the court. 
 
4. 
Any person appearing remotely must mute the person’s 
microphone when joining the proceeding and keep the 
microphone muted until instructed otherwise by the court. 
 
5. 
If a witness appears remotely, the party calling the witness must 
ensure the witness has a functioning camera and microphone 
and has tested the internet connection before the hearing. The 
oath will be administered in accordance with Florida Rule of 
General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.530.

IV. 
Submission of Proposed Orders and Judgments 
 
A. 
Format and Submissions Method 
 
1. 
Judge Patterson accepts proposed orders through the e-filing 
portal. 
 
2. 
The proposed order must be submitted through the e-portal, 
using the “Submit Proposed Order(s) to Judiciary (Court)” option 
on the e-filing map page. 
 
3. 
When submitting a proposed order: 
a. 
Order must be in DJMCA format. E-mail the judicial 
assistant if instructions are needed regarding DJMCA 
format and/or attaching exhibits. 
b. 
Orders must not contain fill-in blanks. 
c. 
Orders must not contain sections breaks. 
d. 
Include the following statement in all submitted 
orders as the last paragraph: “The party who 
submitted this proposed order to the Court for 
approval is required to serve a copy of the signed 
order upon any person(s) not registered to receive 
service via the e-portal.” 
e. 
Proposed orders not complying with the above will 
be rejected. 
f. 
A cover letter in .pdf format must accompany 
proposed orders. 
g. 
The proposed order must be submitted in .docx 
(Word) format. 
h. 
If you convert a .pdf file to a .docx file, read every 
character in the proposed order for accuracy before 
submitting to the Court. 
 
4. 
Self-represented litigants excused from e-mail service may 
submit proposed orders to the court by U.S. mail or hand-
delivered to the judicial office. 
 
B. 
Deadline for Submissions 
 
1. 
Proposed orders must be submitted no later than fourteen (14) 
days after any hearing.

VII. 
Emergency and Other Urgent Matters 
 
A. 
Requirements 
 
1. 
If a party believes there is a factual basis for setting an emergency 
hearing, a detailed motion setting forth the following must be 
filed: (1) the issues to be resolved, (2) reasons why an emergency 
hearing is necessary, and (3) the amount of time needed for each 
party’s presentation. 
 
B. 
Scheduling 
 
1. 
If the court determines that an emergency exists, a hearing will 
be scheduled unilaterally by the court. All parties shall make 
themselves available for the emergency hearing, barring exigent 
circumstances. 
 
VIII. Pretrial Procedures and Conferences

C. 
Certificate of Good Faith Conferral 
 
1. 
Before filing any motion, except as noted in Fla. R. Civ. P. 1.202, 
the moving party shall confer with counsel for the opposing party 
in a good faith effort to resolve the issues raised by the motion, 
and the motion shall contain a statement certifying that the 
moving party has conferred with opposing counsel and that 
counsel have been unable to agree on the resolution of the motion 
(the “Certificate”). 
 
2. 
The term “confer” as used herein, means a substantive 
conversation either in person or by telephone in a good faith effort 
to resolve the motion without court action and does not envision 
an exchange of ultimatums by e-mail, fax, or letter. Certification 
that counsel has attempted to confer with opposing counsel is 
not sufficient. The court may sua sponte deny motions that fail 
to include an appropriate and complete certificate under this 
section. 
 
3. 
The certificate shall set forth the date of the conference, the 
names of the participating attorneys, and the specific results 
achieved. It shall be the responsibility of counsel for the movant 
to arrange for the conference. Counsel are expected to respond 
promptly to inquiries and communication from opposing counsel. 
Repeated failure or refusal of a party or attorney to so confer 
should be set out with specificity in the certificate. 
 
4. 
No conference, therefore, no certificate, is required for 
appropriate ex parte motions, uncontested motions, judgment on 
the pleadings, summary judgment, or other dispositive motions. 
 
5. 
A party alleging that a pleading fails to state a cause of action will 
confer with counsel for the opposing party before moving to 
dismiss, and, upon request of the other party will stipulate to an 
order permitting the filing of a curative amended pleading in lieu 
of filing a motion to dismiss.
