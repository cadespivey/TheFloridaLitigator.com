A. Communications with the Judicial Office

Method of Communication: All filings must be e-filed through the e-Portal. All communications with the judicial office be submitted by e-mail to Jessica.Walker@flcourts1.gov. The subject line must contain the case number, case name, and relevant matter (e.g., 2024 001234 SC – Doe v. Doe).

Ex parte Communications: All communications with the judicial office must comply with Canon 3 of the Code of Judicial Conduct, which prohibits a judge from initiating, permitting, or considering ex parte communications and from considering other communications outside the presence of the parties concerning a pending or impending proceeding, unless authorized by law. All parties must be copied on any e-mail directed to the judicial office, unless an ex parte communication is authorized by law.

Unsolicited Communications: Unsolicited communications from non-parties will not be considered by the court. Parties may only contact the judicial office in accordance with these practices and procedures.

E-Filing Portal Contact Information: All attorneys and self-represented litigants must provide an e-mail address to receive signed orders electronically, unless excused pursuant to Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.516. It is the responsibility of attorneys and self-represented litigants to update their contact information using Form 2.603 any time there is a change in the e-mail account registered for electronic service.

Response to Inquiries: The Code of Judicial Conduct prohibits judicial assistants from making substantive inquiries of the judge on behalf of any attorney or litigant involved in any action. Judicial assistants are neither qualified nor able to provide legal advice to anyone. They may assist with procedural and scheduling inquiries only.

B. Scheduling Procedures

Court Schedule:

A motion must be filed and docketed before a request for hearing time may be submitted. Be sure to schedule adequate time for the motion to be heard. However, please be mindful of the efficient operation of the Court and DO NOT set a hearing for a definitive time slot if the matter is likely to require more time.

Scheduling Zoom Hearings:

It is the responsibility of the moving party to set the motion for hearing. Hearings must be scheduled via email with the Judicial Assistant.

You MUST coordinate the hearing with opposing counsel and/or pro se litigants prior to submitting your request. When you submit a request for hearing time, you are declaring that you have coordinated the date and time with all parties. Good faith cooperation is expected from counsel, their support staff, and pro se litigants. If after 3 attempts on separate days to coordinate a hearing, the opposing party does not cooperate or respond, the requesting party may unilaterally set a hearing giving at least two weeks’ notice. The Notice of Hearing must indicate that the hearing was unilaterally set after 3 attempts on separate days pursuant to these instructions. The Notice must also include the efforts the moving party undertook to coordinate the hearing prior to taking unilateral action.

If assistance is needed, the scheduling party should e-mail judicial assistant Jessica Walker at Jessica.Walker@flcourts1.gov, copying opposing counsel and any self-represented litigant, to coordinate the scheduling of the hearing.

Scheduling In-Person or Hybrid Hearings: In-Person or Hybrid Hearings (where one party appears in-person and the other appears via Zoom) must be scheduled via e-mail with the judicial assistant at Jessica.Walker@flcourts1.gov. All parties should be copied on any e-mail to avoid ex parte communications.

Notice of Hearing:

A notice of hearing must be filed and served immediately after reserving hearing time. Do not “cross notice” a hearing without coordinating express authorization with opposing counsel and the Judicial Assistant. The Court will not address any motions which have not been properly scheduled and noticed.

A notice of hearing involving any remote appearance must list the judicial Zoom meeting ID (733 872 1188) as well as the call-in number (312.626.6799) for individuals without the ability to appear via video.

All notices of hearing must contain the ADA notification required by Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.540.

Submission Deadlines: The court must receive all materials for the hearing no later than five (5) business days before the hearing.

Order of Proceedings: The court will determine the order of proceedings based on the type of proceedings and who is present.

Continuance Procedure: Continuances are disfavored and will be granted only upon good cause shown. A request for continuance must be submitted at least five days prior to the scheduled court date. Except for good cause shown, the motion must be signed by the party requesting the continuance, as required by Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.545(e).

Cancelling Hearings: If it is determined that a scheduled hearing is no longer necessary, you must notify the Judicial Assistant immediately via email. A Notice of Cancellation must be e-filed and a courtesy copy should be emailed to the judicial assistant so that the hearing can be removed from the Court schedule and that time can be offered to another party in another case.

C. Remote Appearance

Remote Appearance Procedure: On occasion, the court maintains a hybrid virtual courtroom, allowing parties to appear either in person or remotely, as provided by Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.530. Requests to use communication technology for a scheduled in person docket or hearing date must be made by motion.

Platform Used: The court uses Zoom Teleconference for remote appearances.

Platform Meeting ID#: 733 872 1188.

Requirements: Please note the following requirements for any person appearing remotely.

Dress code and behavior are the same on Zoom as if you were appearing in the courtroom.

Those appearing via Zoom should ensure that they are familiar with the platform and shall sign in early to ensure timely appearance. As some hearings are set for as little as ten minutes, failure to appear on time may result in a default judgment against you, waiver of objection(s) or other sanctions.

Please ensure that your username reflects your full name in lieu of a nickname or screenname.

A private location that is quiet and free from distractions is required. Under no circumstances will a participant be permitted to appear remotely while driving.

Your camera must be active when joining the proceeding and kept on until instructed otherwise by the court.

Your microphone must be muted until instructed otherwise by the court.

If a witness appears remotely, the party calling the witness must ensure the witness has a functioning camera and microphone and has tested the internet connection before the hearing. The oath will be administered in accordance with Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.530.

D. Submission of Orders and Judgments

Format: All proposed orders must be submitted in Microsoft Word format and be accompanied by a cover letter either (1) certifying that all parties agree to the order or (2) containing a statement identifying any disagreement of the parties as to the proposed order. Proposed orders must include the names and e-mail addresses of all parties who will need to receive e-service of the order once signed.

Submission Method: E-Filing Portal

Deadline for Submissions: Proposed orders must be submitted within 10 days after any hearing, unless specifically stated otherwise by the Judge during the hearing.

Pre-Trial Conference Judgments: If a proposed judgment is based on a defendant’s failure to appear for a Pre-Trial Conference, please include that information in the proposed judgment. Additionally, please include the date and type of process-service afforded to the defendant.

Orders/judgments to pro se litigants: If a case involves a pro se party without a designated email address, please include the physical mailing address in the proposed order/judgment to ensure proper distribution of copies.

E. Courtesy Copies of Case Law and Other Documents: Courtesy copies of case law for consideration at a motion hearing should be provided at least three (3) business days prior to the scheduled hearing to the judicial assistant via email or hand delivery.

F. Emergency and Other Urgent Matters

Requirements: If a party believes there is a factual basis for setting an emergency hearing, a detailed motion setting forth the following must be filed: (1) the issues to be resolved, (2) reasons why an emergency hearing is necessary, and (3) the amount of time needed for each party’s presentation.

Scheduling: If the court determines that an emergency exists, a hearing will be scheduled unilaterally by the court. All parties shall make themselves available for the emergency hearing, barring exigent circumstances.

G. Exhibits for Evidentiary Proceedings

Submission Method:
For Zoom and/or Hybrid Hearings - Proposed exhibits must be e-filed with the Clerk through the e-filing portal five (5) days prior to the remote appearance. Courtesy copies for the Court may be submitted electronically by e-mail to the division e-mail account.

For In-Person Hearings - All attorneys and self-represented litigants must bring sufficient copies of each exhibit for the clerk, the court, and each party to review during the hearing or trial. Digital evidence must be contained on media appropriate for permanent retention by the Clerk of Courts. Exhibits must be labeled in the following format: ‘Petitioner/Plaintiff 1’ or ‘Respondent/Defendant A.’

H. Pretrial Procedures and Conferences

Case Management Conference: Any party may request a case management conference (CMC) when a case requires. The court strongly encourages the early use of CMC in more complex cases, multiple-party litigation, or any case that might benefit from court intervention. Unless excused by the court in advance, all CMCs are mandatory for attorneys and self-represented litigants. Parties represented by counsel are not required to appear at a CMC.

Status Conference: Any party may request a status conference when a case requires.

Scheduling: The moving party may schedule the case management conference or status conference through the online scheduling platform after coordinating with counsel for the other party or the pro-se party.

I. Setting Case for Trial

Procedure: Unless otherwise determined by the Court, a party may file a notice for non-jury trial to set the case for trial. A party must file a Motion for Case Management Conference before seeking to set the case for Jury Trial. The Court prefers that a trial be held in-person. However, a non-jury trial can be held via zoom when appropriate to the scale of the anticipated evidence. Be mindful of the Court’s docket with a mindful estimation of the amount of time needed for the trial to be completed. Any objection to the Notice must be filed within ten (10) days. Please coordinate with the Court to schedule the trial according to the procedures outlined above.

K. Other Division Procedures

ADA Accommodations: If you are a person with a disability who needs any accommodation in order to participate in this proceeding, you are entitled, at no cost to you, to the provision of certain assistance. Please contact Court Administration, ADA Liaison, Santa Rosa County, 4025 Avalon Blvd, Milton, Florida, 32583, Phone (850) 623-3159, Fax (850) 982-0602, ADA.SantaRosa@flcourts1.gov at least 7 days before your scheduled court appearance, or immediately upon receiving this notification if the time before the scheduled appearance is less than 7 days; if you are hearing or voice impaired, call 711.

Interpreter Requests: If an interpreter is needed for a hearing or trial, please contact the Clerk’s office or the Judicial Assistant to make interpreter arrangements.
