DIVISION PREFERENCES (click for a printable version)

This is intended as a brief overview of policies regarding hearings and procedural matters. Your courtesies in adhering to these policies will be greatly appreciated and will make our system one which runs more smoothly and efficiently for all involved.

SCHEDULING CIVIL HEARINGS

1 HOUR OR LESS

Any motion(s) must be filed and docketed before scheduling hearing time.

Hearing times must be scheduled via OnceHub.

Five-minute time slots should be used for non-evidentiary motions that are either uncontested or can be heard within a short time span such as Motions to Withdraw (without stipulation) or Uncontested MSJ/NJT on Foreclosures.

Be sure to schedule adequate time for the motion(s) to be heard.DO NOT set a hearing for 15 minutes if the matter will, in all likelihood, require more than 15 minutes.

Please be mindful of the fact that there is probably another hearing scheduled after your hearing, and that if your hearing runs longer than scheduled you are affecting other attorneys and litigants.Be respectful of the time of others and the Court.

If you are scheduling multiple motions for the same date, please DO NOT schedule each motion separately through OnceHub.Doing so will create a separate ZOOM meeting for each motion with 5 minute breaks between each one, not to mention quite a bit of confusionYou will need to determine how much time is needed to hear those motions and schedule accordingly.

You MUST coordinate the hearing with opposing counsel and/or pro se litigant prior to submitting your request.When you submit a request for hearing time you are indicating that you have coordinated the date and time with all parties.

Good faith cooperation is expected both from counsel, their support staff and pro se litigants. If after 3 attempts on separate days have been made to coordinate a hearing and counsel does not cooperate or respond, the requesting party may unilaterally set a hearing giving at least two weeks’ notice to the opposing counsel who failed to cooperate or respond. Efforts to coordinate the hearing must be noted on the Notice of Hearing.

Once the coordinated hearing time is submitted, you will receive an automatic email from “First Judicial Circuit of Florida” confirming the hearing date/time/ZOOM information.

Evidentiary hearings, regardless of amount of time, will require IN-PERSON appearance.

For IN-PERSON hearings please schedule via OnceHub.OnceHub automatically schedules hearings by ZOOM.Once the hearing has been scheduled, please email tressie.leatherman@flcourts1.gov to have the hearing converted to in-person.

1.5 to 2 HOURS

Hearings scheduled for more than one hour will require IN-PERSON appearance.

Please use OnceHub to see the dates/times available that would accommodate a 2-hour hearing. Select 60 minutes then click through until you find a block that has two hours available.

See below image for example.Options for 2 hours in the morning would be 9:00 & 10:00, 9:15 & 10:15, or 9:30 & 10:30 (9:45 would not be an option because there is not a 10:45 option) or afternoon at 1:00 & 2:00, 1:15 & 2:15, 1:30 & 2:30, 1:45 & 2:45, or 2:00 & 3:00.

DO NOT PROCEED TO CONFIRM THROUGH ONCEHUB if hearing will be 1.5-2 hours. Please email me once everyone has agreed on one or two dates and I will remove it from OnceHub and confirm the date/time.

HEARINGS OVER 2 HOURS, PLEASE EMAIL Tressie.leatherman@flcourts1.gov

NOTICE OF HEARING

A COURTESY COPY OF THE NOTICE OF HEARING MUST BE PROVIDED TO THE COURT FOR THE HEARING TO BE COMPLETELY CONFIRMED AND ADDED TO THE CALENDAR.

All the information needed for the Notice of Hearing (NOH) will be provided in the OnceHub automatic email confirmation from “First Judicial Circuit of Florida”, including date/time/ZOOM information.

Please note this is a Santa Rosa County case, which is in CT (Central Time Zone), therefore all notices MUST be for CT.You are welcome to include counsel’s time zone in the NOH if it differs from the Court’s.

If the scheduled hearing is via ZOOM, the ZOOM information provided in the confirmation MUST be included in the NOH.Confirm the ZOOM information on the NOH is the same as provided in the confirmation email.

Do not “cross notice” a hearing without having express authorization from opposing counsel and Judge Duncan’s office. The judge will not hear a motion(s) which has not been properly scheduled and noticed.

All notices of court proceedings to be held in a public facility must contain the statement below in bold face, 14-point Times New Roman or Courier font.

If you are a person with a disability who needs any accommodation in order to participate in this proceeding, you are entitled, at no cost to you, to the provision of certain assistance. Please contact:

NOTICE OF CANCELLATION

Only the party who set the hearing may cancel it, and it is that party’s responsibility to file a written notice of cancellation of the hearing.A courtesy copy must be provided to the Court and all participants, including any pro se party, and court reporter if one was secured.

If a case or issue is resolved, please notify Judge Duncan’s office immediately and cancel all scheduled hearings so that time can be made available for other hearings. Valuable calendar time is lost because notice of cancellation is not given in a timely fashion.

MOTIONS

Motions to Compel and Motions for Extension

Unfortunately, our case load typically does not allow for these motions to be heard as expeditiously as they should be in order to move the cases to a resolution.Usually, the motion wouldn’t be able to be scheduled and heard until well past the amount of time the party is asking, creating a de facto extension for longer than requested to begin with.

The parties may efile a stipulated order including the number of days agreed to or submit a proposed order with the number of days blank for Judge Duncan to decide.

Motions to Compel that are filed due to NO responses will now be addressed without a hearing.Please efile proposed order compelling a response within thirty (30) days and reserving jurisdiction to award attorney fees if applicable.If the motion is filed based on objections, a hearing may be scheduled.

Certificate of Good Faith Conference

Before filing any motion, except as noted in paragraph c, the moving party shall confer with counsel for the opposing party in a good faith effort to resolve the issues raised by the motion, and the motion shall contain a statement certifying that the moving party has conferred with opposing counsel and that counsel have been unable to agree on the resolution of the motion (the “Certificate”).

a. The term “confer” as used herein, means a substantive conversation either in person or by telephone in a good faith effort to resolve the motion without court action and does not envision an exchange of ultimatums by email, fax, or letter. Certification that counsel has attempted to confer with opposing counsel is not sufficient. The court may sua sponte deny motions that fail to include an appropriate and complete certificate under this section.

b. The certificate shall set forth the date of the conference, the names of the participating attorneys, and the specific results achieved. It shall be the responsibility of counsel for the movant to arrange for the conference. Counsel is expected to respond promptly to inquiries and communication from opposing counsel. Repeated failure or refusal of a party or attorney to so confer should be set out with specificity in the certificate.

c. No conference, therefore no certificate, is required for appropriate ex-parte motions, uncontested motions, judgment on the pleadings, summary judgment, or other dispositive motions.

d. A party alleging that a pleading fails to state a cause of action will confer with counsel for the opposing party before moving to dismiss, and, upon request of the other party will stipulate to an order permitting the filing of a curative amended pleading in lieu of filing a motion to dismiss.

PROPOSED ORDERS & HEARING MATERIAL

Proposed orders and hearing materials are submitted through the Florida Courts E-Filing Portal.This is done via the “E-Filing Map”.

You will find Santa Rosa under “Select a Filing Jurisdiction”.Check the “Submit Proposed Order(s) to Judiciary (Court)” option then “File Now”.From there you will proceed to enter the case information, select “Duncan, J. Scott” and proceed to “Next” or the Documents tab.

PROPOSED ORDERS

Under the Documents tab, please click “Order” to attach and submit proposed orders.

Judge Duncan DOES NOT require a cover letter, although you may do so if there is important information regarding the proposed order that needs to be relayed.

All proposed orders should be titled the same as the title of the order you are submitting.Also, it is not necessary to include “proposed” in the title (i.e. Order on Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel).

Proposed orders are to be submitted in Word format.

In the event the proposed order requires an attachment that cannot be accomplished via Word, you may use the “Signed Proposal” option, which will allow filing of a PDF.

All proposed orders must include the last paragraph (before DONE and ORDERED) the following statement:

In cases wherein one party is unrepresented (pro se), it is the responsibility of the sole attorney in the case to serve within five business days this [order or judgment [upon any pro se party who does not have access to and is not a registered user of the Florida Court e-Filing Portal.

Proposed orders are to be submitted with Judge Duncan’s preferred signature block as shown below.

DO NOT use DJMCA format codes (DDDD, JJJJ, CCCC) for the signature block.

Once the proposed order or judgment is signed, it will be e-filed. It is the responsibility of the submitting party/counsel to provide copies to any party not included on the service list. Do not include a certificate of service to be completed by the judicial assistant.

To check the status of a specific proposed order to see if a ruling has been made by the Judge, you should first check the clerk’s system to see if it has been docketed. If the order is not found, an email to the JA at tressie.leatherman@flcourts1.gov can be sent (allow a minimum of five (5) business days to process proposed orders) to inquire the status of said order.

HEARING MATERIAL

Under the Documents tab, please click “Hearing Materials – JUDGE DUNCAN ONLY” to attach and submit any documents that counsel would like to present for judicial review (i.e. caselaw or proposed exhibits). All submissions must be titled with the date of hearing and party.For example, 01/01/26 Plaintiff or Defendant-John Doe if more than one defendant.

Please DO NOT submit courtesy copies of anything that has already been filed in the case.

FORECLOSURE CASES

All originals documents (i.e.. note and mortgage) must be filed with the Clerk of Courts Prior to scheduling a Motion for Summary Judgment or Non-Jury Trial in a foreclosure case.

Motions for Summary Judgment

Once a Motion for Summary Judgment is filed, you may schedule the hearing via OnceHub.If uncontested, you may schedule for a 5-minute hearing.After scheduling, please file the NOH as instructed under the NOTICE OF HEARING section above.Please wait until 1-2 days prior to the hearing to file the proposed Final Judgment. see PROPOSED ORDERS

Non-Jury Trials for Foreclosures

Once a Notice for Trial is filed, you may schedule via OnceHub.If uncontested, you may schedule for a 5-minute hearing.After scheduling you must e-file a proposed Order Scheduling Non-Jury Trial, including the date/time/ZOOM information provided in the email confirmation.Please wait until 1-2 days prior to the hearing to file the proposed Final Judgment.see PROPOSED ORDERS

TRIALS

Setting a Civil Jury/Judge Trial

Please file a Notice for Readiness for Trial once the case is ready to be set for a Jury/Judge trial.After filing the notice, please schedule a Case Management Conference via OnceHub.Case Management Conferences are scheduled for 15 minutes.

Any objections to the notice for trial will be heard at the Case Management Conference.

All parties must have their calendars available during the Case Management Conference in order to coordinate a trial date. Depending on how much work has been completed in the case, the Court may determine that a specific trial date is not yet necessary, and that such a date will be set after a subsequent Case Management Conference.However, even if a specific trial date is not given, the Court may still set discovery and mediation deadlines.

Once the trial date has been set, the Court will enter an Order for Pre-Trial Conference and Setting Trial.

The Court does not determine the order of priority of civil jury trials until approximately 30 days before the trial date, so counsel should prepare as if their case will be tried on the set date.

Mediation must be attempted before commencement of the trial. Counsel is encouraged to attempt mediation without waiting for a Court order.

If your case is set for trial and settles, notify the J.A. immediately and also cancel any hearings that may be scheduled. The mere filing of a settlement documents with the Clerk does not notify the court that a case set for trial has been resolved.
