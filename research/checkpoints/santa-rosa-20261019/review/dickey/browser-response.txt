If the judicial assistant is unable to substantively respond within THREE business days, your message will be acknowledged as received with an indication of when to expect a substantive response and alternate contact for immediate assistance.\nWhen the judicial assistant is out of the office, your message will be acknowledged as received with an indication of when to expect a substantive response and an alternate contact for immediate assistance.\n\nB. Scheduling Procedures\n\nScheduling Hearings:\nScheduling Motion Hearings for Less Than One Hour:\nCounsel may schedule a motion hearing of 60 minutes or less using OnceHub (online hearing application), at Judge Darlene F. Dickey - Online scheduling. This application is currently not available for parties not represented by an attorney.\nNOTE: The motion must be properly filed in the court record prior to scheduling the matter for a hearing.\nThe moving party is responsible for confirming the opposing party’s availability before selecting a date and time on OnceHub. Do not ADD motions to an existing hearing UNLESS opposing counsel agrees, time permits, and the party has contacted the judicial assistant to confirm the additional motion.\n\nScheduling Final Hearings or Hearings Exceeding One Hour:\nALL Final Hearings, no matter the length, and other hearings expected to last longer than one hour may be scheduled using one of the following methods:\narrange a conference call with opposing counsel’s office first, then contact the JA at 850-981-5541 office; or\nemail the judicial assistant, copying all parties without attorneys and all attorneys- include (a) the request for hearing, (b) the title of the motion at issue, (c) the date the motion was filed, and (d) the amount of time needed; or\nobtain dates by phone from the judicial assistant to coordinate with opposing party/parties and then contact the judicial assistant once an agreement is reached.\nPlease provide the following information: case number, title of motion, date it was filed or clerk tag number, and length of time requested for this hearing. Time requested is split equally between both sides. The Court does not offer hearing time for motions that are not properly filed in the court record. It is the moving party’s responsibility to coordinate the hearing with the opposing party and then let the judicial assistant know the agreed-upon date.\nDue to the high volume of emails, DO NOT include the judicial assistant in the coordination process, or the “back and forth” between the people scheduling the hearings. Once a date is agreed upon, the moving party must advise the judicial assistant so that the selected date and time can be confirmed. The JA can be reached at noelle.martin@flcourts1.gov or 850-981-5541 to confirm that the hearing date and time are still available. Please do not leave a voicemail to confirm a hearing date, as the date may no longer be available.\nNOTE: The judicial assistant offers first available dates and times to everyone until hearing time has been filled. The judicial assistant does not hold dates and times and hearings are confirmed on a first come/first served basis. Protecting hearing time on the Judge’s calendar happens only after the judicial assistant has confirmed the date and time. Additional motions may be added to an existing hearing ONLY if all parties agree, time permits, and the party has contacted the judicial assistant to confirm the additional motion.\n\nNotice of Hearing:\nThe moving party must prepare, serve, and file the Notice of Hearing. The Notice should include: (1) the title of each motion scheduled; (2) the date of filing for each motion scheduled; (3) the date and time of the hearing, along with reference to the time zone; (4) the length of the hearing.\nThe Judge does not automatically receive a copy of every motion filed. The moving party is responsible for requesting hearings on motions filed in the court file and to coordinate that hearing with the other parties and the JA. Merely filing a motion with the Clerk will not bring the matter before the Judge.\nAll notices of hearing must contain the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) notification required by Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.540.\nUpon determining a hearing is no longer necessary, contact the JA immediately by calling and emailing the judicial assistant at noelle.martin@flcourts1.gov and 850-981-5541. The subject line of the email should indicate the intent to cancel a hearing and the date of hearing. The ONLY way this office knows of a cancellation is through your notification. If the hearing is cancelled far enough in advance, the time can be made available to others.\nThe moving party shall also file and serve a Notice of Cancellation to all attorneys and any unrepresented parties.\n\nC. Remote Appearance\n\nRemote Appearance Procedure:\nThe court maintains a hybrid virtual courtroom, allowing parties to appear either in person or remotely, as provided by Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.530. Requests to use communication technology for an appearance must be made by motion. The Court uses Zoom for remote appearances. A written motion by a party to present testimony through Zoom must set forth good cause why the testimony should be allowed by Zoom and must specify whether each party consents to the form requested.\n2.530(b)(1)Non-Evidentiary Proceedings. A court official must grant a motion to use communication technology for a non-evidentiary proceeding scheduled for 30 minutes or less unless the court official determines that good cause exists to deny the motion.\nThe Notice of Hearing shall include the Meeting ID Number which can be obtained from the Judicial Assistant.\nRefer to the Rules of Remote Appearances at this location: Court Hearings by ZOOM | First Judicial Circuit of Florida\nPlatform Used: Zoom\nPlatform Meeting ID#: Please contact the Judicial Assistant for the Meeting ID Number.\n\nD. Submission of Orders and Judgments\n\nFormat: Microsoft Word\nSubmission Method: All proposed orders are to be submitted via the ePortal, by way of the “Proposed Order” button and selecting Santa Rosa in the drop-down list and selecting the correct Judge. Proposed orders must be in MS Word format. Please do not submit a proposed order to the Court for signing until all parties have reviewed and approved the language and its entry.\nDeadline for Submissions: Unless otherwise stated by the Court during a hearing, any orders memorializing the Court’s rulings made during a hearing shall be submitted within 30 days of the hearing. Some orders are needed sooner than 30 days, and the attorneys should prioritize the proposed orders based upon the subject matter at issue.\n[Other Procedures Relating to Submission of Orders and Judgments]: PLEASE DO NOT SUBMIT COMPETING ORDERS. If the parties cannot agree on a proposed order, the non-drafting attorney shall send a letter to the Court explaining the disagreement.\n\nE. Courtesy Copies of Case Law and Other Documents\n\nWhen Required: The Court appreciates receiving case law and legal authority prior to hearings to be as prepared as possible for each hearing. If provided to the Court, parties must also provide the same documents or authority to all other parties.\n\nF. Emergency and Other Urgent Matters\n\nRequirements: If a party believes there is a factual basis for setting an emergency hearing, a detailed motion setting forth the following must be filed: (1) the issues to be resolved, (2) reasons why an emergency hearing is necessary, and (3) the amount of time needed for each party’s presentation.\nScheduling: If the Court finds an emergency exists, the hearing will be set at the next available time on the Court’s calendar. All parties shall make themselves available for the emergency hearing, barring exigent circumstances.\n\nG. Exhibits for Evidentiary Proceedings\n\nFormat: The Court prefers that documentary evidence/exhibits are NOT placed into 3-ring binders, binding combs, spiral binding or other methods that impair the Court’s ability to easily separate the exhibits for presentation to the Clerk of Court following the hearing. Do not expect the court to use its resources to print your notebooks, exhibits, case law, etc.\nDeadline for Submissions: Exhibits and caselaw must be submitted at least 48 hours prior to hearing unless otherwise addressed in your scheduling order.\n[Other Procedures Relating to Exhibits for Evidentiary Proceedings]: Proposed Final Judgments for Dissolution of Marriage by Special Affidavit should only be submitted after the 10-day waiting period has expired, and if there are no objections. Please note that you must use the approved Santa Rosa County checklist form. This checklist must be e-filed, and all documents listed on the checklist must be filed before the Court can enter the final judgment. Santa Rosa County’s approved checklist can be found here.\n\nH. Pretrial Procedures and Conferences\n\nCase Management Conference and Status Conference:\nAny party may request a Case Management Conference, and the Court may require a Case Management and/or Pretrial Conference. See Rule 12.200, Family Law Rules of Procedure.\nThe court strongly encourages the early use of CMCs in more complex cases, multiple-party litigation, or any case that might benefit from court intervention.\nAny request for a CMC or status conference must articulate the reasons for the necessity of the conference. If the court grants the request for a CMC or status conference, the moving party must schedule the CMC or status conference pursuant to the procedures noted under the above Scheduling Procedures.\n\nMotions to Withdraw and Substitutions of Counsel: Written consent of the client must be filed with the Clerk of Court and opposing counsel (if any) must have no objection to the withdrawal/substitution. Rule 2.505, Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration. If the client has not signed consent or opposing counsel has an objection to the withdrawal, a hearing is required.\n\nI. Setting Case for Trial\n\nProcedure:\nThe Court will not schedule a final hearing unless a notice that the action is at issue has been filed pursuant to Rule 12.440, Family Law Rules of Procedure. The notice must include an estimate of the time required, indicate whether the trial is on the original action or a subsequent proceeding, and it should be provided to the Judicial Assistant.\nThe file will be reviewed to determine if all necessary documents and information have been filed. The JA will notify the parties of any deficiencies that need to be resolved before providing potential trial dates.\nThe Court shall enter an order setting the action for trial and fixing a date for trial, along with any scheduling or pretrial orders the Court finds are appropriate. Rule 12.440(c), Family Law Rules of Procedure.\n\nJ. Forms\n\nAccess: Florida Supreme Court Family Law forms are available using this link: Family Law Forms - Florida Courts\n\nK. The following are guidelines outlining time-honored customs and practices and are recognized in the Rules of the United States District Court for the Northern District of Florida.\nAddress all remarks to the court, not to opposing counsel.\nAvoid disparaging personal remarks or acrimony toward opposing counsel and remain wholly detached from any ill feeling between the litigants or witnesses.\nRefer to all persons, including witnesses, other counsel, and the parties, by their surnames and not by their first or given names.\nCounsel should request permission before approaching the bench.\nUnless opposing counsel has previously been shown exhibits, any exhibit offered in evidence should, at the time of such offer, be handed to opposing counsel.\nIn making objections, counsel should state only the legal grounds for the objection and should withhold all further comment or argument unless elaboration is requested by the court.\nCounsel shall admonish and discourage all persons (parties, witnesses and observers) from making gestures, facial expressions, audible comments, or the like, as manifestations of approval or disapproval during the testimony of witnesses, or at any other time.
