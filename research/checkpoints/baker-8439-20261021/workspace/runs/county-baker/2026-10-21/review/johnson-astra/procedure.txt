Judge Julie C. Johnson’s Division Procedures
Baker County Court
Communications with the Judicial Office
Method of Communication:
All communications to the judicial office must be submitted by e-mail to
crewsk@circuit8.org
. The subject line must contain the case number, case name, and relevant matter (e.g., 2024 DR 001234 SC – Doe v. Doe – 2-Hour Hearing Requested.)
Please note:
A judicial assistant is
not
authorized to provide legal advice.
Ex parte Communications:
All communications with the judicial office must comply with Canon 3 of the Code of Judicial Conduct, which prohibits a judge from initiating, permitting, or considering ex parte communications and from considering other communications outside the presence of the parties concerning a pending or impending proceeding, unless authorized by law. All parties must be copied on any e-mail directed to the judicial office, unless an ex parte communication is authorized by law.
Unsolicited Communications:
Unsolicited communications from non-parties will not be considered by the court. Parties may only contact the judicial office in accordance with these practices and procedures.
E-Filing Portal Contact Information:
All attorneys and self-represented litigants must provide an e-mail address to receive signed orders electronically, unless excused. Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.516. It is the responsibility of attorneys and self-represented litigants to update their contact information using Form 2.603 any time there is a change in the e-mail account registered for electronic service.
Response to Inquiries:
Judicial Assistants are
not
authorized to provide legal advice. When you contact the Judge’s office please know letters, e-mails and other correspondence are all public record and will be recorded.
If you have not received a response to an inquiry within 48 hours, you may send a follow-up email.
Out-of-Office Procedure for Judicial Assistant:
Follow the directions of the out-of-office email response if an email was sent, or in the event of an Emergency, you may contact the Clerk of Court’s Office or Court Administration.
Scheduling Procedures
Court Schedule:
Master Calendar – Baker County Court
Scheduling Hearings:
Please email
Crewsk@circuit8.org
to request a hearing time. The email request should include:
Case number
Style of the case
A courtesy copy of the motion(s) or petitions(s) being set
The
total
amount of time requested
Whether the opposing party is represented by counsel
Our office does not hold suggested dates for hearings and a hearing is not scheduled on the Court’s calendar unless you receive confirmation from the judicial assistant.
Once a motion or petition is scheduled, any other motions or matters may NOT be noticed at the same time reserved for the first motion unless prior approval has been obtained from the judicial assistant.
Notice of Hearing:
A notice of hearing must be filed and served immediately after reserving hearing time. A courtesy copy of the notice of hearing should be emailed to the judicial assistant. A notice of hearing involving any remote appearance must list the judicial Zoom credentials. All notices of hearing must contain the ADA notification required by Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.540.
Submission Deadlines:
The Court must receive all materials for the hearing no later than three (3) business days before the hearings.
Continuance Procedure:
Motions to Continue received less than 24 hours prior to the court event MAY not be ruled upon prior to the court event. Parties are expected to appear if an Order to Continue is not entered.
Cancelling Hearings:
You must cancel hearings by notifying the judicial assistant immediately. You must also immediately file and serve a notice of cancellation on opposing counsel and any self-represented litigant.
Remote Appearance
Remote Appearance Procedure:
For some dockets (
Criminal, Tobacco, and Truancy
), the court maintains a hybrid virtual courtroom, allowing parties to appear either in person or remotely, as provided by Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.530. Requests to use communication technology for an appearance must be made by motion at least 48 hours prior to the scheduled hearing. A courtesy copy of the filed Motion must be e-mailed to the judicial assistant at
crewsk@circuit8.org
and a proposed order submitted via the e-portal.
For
Criminal Court
, Zoom appearances are
not
permitted for pre-trial conferences, change of pleas, evidentiary hearings, motion hearings scheduled for more than 30 minutes, jury selection, trials, trial status conferences, or trial meetings.
For
Small Claims, County Civil, and Traffic cases,
the dockets are conducted
solely
via Zoom, unless the party requests to appear in person no less than 24 hours prior to the scheduled hearing. Requests to appear in person should be made by notifying the Judicial Assistant at
crewsk@circuit8.org
.
Platform Used:
The court uses Zoom for remote appearances.
Platform Meeting ID#:
Please contact the Judicial Assistant to obtain the platform meeting ID# and password.
Requirements:
Any party granted a remote appearance
must
:
Have a stable internet connection and a charged device with functioning camera, sound and microphone capabilities;
Be in a private location that is quiet and free from distractions;
Under no circumstances will a participant be permitted to appear remotely from a moving vehicle;
Dress and behave professionally in the same manner as if physically present in the courtroom;
Mute the microphone when joining the proceeding and keep the microphone muted until your case is called or instructed otherwise by the court.
If a witness appears remotely, the party calling the witness must ensure the witness has a functioning camera and microphone and has tested the internet the internet before the hearing. The oath will be administered in accordance with Florida Rule of General Practice and Judicial Administration 2.530.
Technology Needs:
For additional information, please review and comply with the Zoom procedures which may be found at:
https://circuit8.org/zoom-guidelines/
Submission of Orders and Judgments
Format:
All proposed orders must include the following coding after the body of the proposed order.  This coding allows our computer system to input the proper dates and signatures for processing.
DONE AND ORDERED
in Chambers at Macclenny, Baker County, Florida on DDDD.
JJJJ
CERTIFICATE OF SERVICE
I HEREBY CERTIFY
that copies have been furnished by U.S. Mail or via filing with the Florida Courts E-Filing Portal on MMMM.
CCCC
AAAA
Submission Method:
Proposed orders and final judgments shall be submitted via the Florida Courts E-Filing Portal (www. https://www.myflcourtaccess.com/). Do not send proposed orders or final judgments by United States Mail or email. All proposed orders or final judgments sent directly to the Court by attorneys will be refused without processing.
If there are parties who have not consented to use of e-mail service, stamped envelopes should be mailed or hand delivered to the Judicial Assistant, Kristina Crews. Please clip the envelopes together, along with a sticky note that indicates the case name and case number. PLEASE DO NOT ADDRESS THE ENVELOPES; LEAVE THE ENVELOPES BLANK BUT BE SURE TO ADD THE PROPER POSTAGE.
Orders will not be entered until the motion has been docketed and imaged by the Clerk. Docketing and imaging by the Clerk may take more than 48 hours from electronic filing.
Deadline for Submissions:
Proposed Orders or Final Judgments shall be submitted to the Court within 10 days after any hearing, unless otherwise instructed by the Court.
Time Sensitive Matters:
For time sensitive matters that require immediate attention, please email
Crewsk@circuit8.org
to advise that a proposed Order has been uploaded to the portal.
Courtesy Copies of Case Law and Other Documents
When Required:
Courtesy copies of case law or other legal authority must be submitted to the court for any evidentiary proceeding.
Format:
PDF format
Submission Method:
Please submit via e-mail to
crewsk@circuit8.org
.
Deadline for Submission:
Courtesy copies must be e-mailed to
Crewsk@circuit8.org
no later than two (2) business days before any evidentiary proceeding. Please also bring a hard copy to the hearing.
Emergency and Other Urgent Matters
Requirements:
If a party believes there is a factual basis for setting an emergency hearing, a detailed motion setting forth the following must be filed: (1) the issues to be resolved, (2) reasons why an emergency hearing is necessary, and (3) the amount of time needed for each party’s presentation.
Scheduling:
If the court determines that an emergency exists, a hearing will be scheduled unilaterally by the court. All parties shall make themselves available for the emergency hearing, barring exigent circumstances.
Exhibits for Evidentiary Proceedings
Submission Method:
You may submit Exhibits to the Judicial Assistant via email as a PDF or Word document if it is less than 50 pages. Exhibits more than 50 pages will need to be mailed to the court in a binder or folder, with identifying tabs, at least three (3) days prior to the hearing. If you intend to present any evidence or documents (including memos and caselaw) to the Court, you must pre-mark all evidence and serve a copy of all documents to all parties prior to the hearing. Parties shall confer prior to the hearing to determine any stipulations as to the admission of evidence and documents.
Format:
If exhibits are for an in-person evidentiary proceeding, exhibits must be submitted to the Clerk of Court in paper format at the hearing. All attorneys and self-represented litigants must bring sufficient copies of each exhibit for the clerk, the court, and each party to review during the hearing or trial. Exhibits must be labeled in the following format: ‘Petitioner/Plaintiff 1’ or ‘Respondent/Defendant A’.
Deadline for Submissions:
If exhibits are to be entered for an evidentiary hearing via the Zoom platform, all exhibits must be received in chambers three (3) days before the evidentiary proceeding. You may submit Exhibits to the Judicial Assistant via email as a PDF or Word document if it is less than 50 pages. Exhibits more than 50 pages will need to be mailed to the court in a binder or folder, with identifying tabs, at least three (3) days prior to the hearing.
Pretrial Procedures and Conferences
Procedures for Civil Cases:
Case Management Conference:
The court may set a case management conference at any time deemed appropriate. In addition, any party may request a case management conference (CMC) when a case requires. The court strongly encourages the early use of CMC in more complex cases, multiple-party litigation, or any case that might benefit from court intervention. Unless excused by the court in advance, all CMCs are mandatory for attorneys and self-represented litigants. Parties represented by counsel are not required to appear at a CMC.
Status Conference:
Any party may request a status conference when a case requires.
Requirements:
Any request for a CMC or status conference must articulate the reasons for the necessity of the conference.
Scheduling:
If the court agrees that a CMC or status conference is required, the court will coordinate a hearing date/time and the moving party shall file a notice of hearing.
Procedures Relating to Small Claims Pretrial Conferences or Mediation:
The parties and/or all attorneys of record
must appear with full authority to mediate
pursuant to the requirements of Florida Rule of Civil Procedure 1.720
Procedures Relating to Criminal Cases:
Case Management Conference:
In-custody Defendants will appear remotely from the jail. A Motion and proposed Order to Transport is required if their attorney requests an in-person appearance.
Pre-Trial Conference:
The Court requires all Defendants and Attorneys of Record to appear in-person for Pre-Trial Conferences. Their appearance may not be waived, unless otherwise approved by the Court. The Court encourages parties to file and schedule all substantive pre-trial motions prior to the Pre-Trial Conference.
Changes of Plea:
Parties should have a completed plea petition with the applicable attachments at the time of the scheduled court event.
Links to these documents are included below
.
Substitution of Counsel:
Stipulations should be signed by the defendant, current and new counsel. New counsel shall file the signed stipulation and Motion and submit a proposed Order via the Florida Courts E-Filing Portal. The Court will require a hearing if all signatures are not present.
Coverage Attorneys:
Pursuant to Administrative Order No. 1.30, “coverage” attorneys must file a notice of appearance as co-counsel before the Court will recognize the attorney as representing that party.
Motion for Continuance:
You must contact opposing counsel to determine if they have an objection prior to filing a Motion for Continuance. The Motion must be filed with the Clerk of Court and must indicate if opposing counsel objects to the continuance. If the continuance is agreed to by all parties, please submit an Agreed Order to the e-portal. If the opposing party objects to the continuance, it may be necessary to set a hearing on the Motion but still upload a proposed order to the e-portal.  Please see the procedure for scheduling a hearing. The Court will evaluate the Motions for Continuance on a case-by-case basis even if the opposing party agrees or does not object to the continuance.
Termination or Modification of Probation:
All Motions for Termination or Modification of Probation shall be filed in the court file and emailed to the Judicial Assistant at
crewsk@circuit8.org
with a copy to the State and the Probation Officer.  Please follow the process for a hearing request.  A Notice of Hearing shall be prepared by the requesting party, e-filed and shared with all parties including the Probation Officer.
Setting Case for Trial
Procedures:
Civil Cases
: File a notice for trial to set the case for trial and notify the judicial assistant that it has been filed. A trial date will be coordinated with the parties.
Small Claims
: Cases not resolved at Pre-Trial Conference/Mediation will be set for trial within 60 days. A trial date will be coordinated with the parties.
Criminal Cases
: Cases are set for trial at the Trial Status Conference. Trial week is the second full week of each month. Jury selection is the Monday following the Trial Status Conference with trial occurring on one of the days during the same week.
Notice Period:
For criminal cases, jury trials are scheduled for the second full week of each month. Jury selection is the Monday of that week, with the trial occurring on one of the days during the same week. A trial calendar is issued after the Trial Status Conference hearing the week prior to the trial week. Counsel should be prepared to discuss availability for trial at the Trial Status Conference and the Court will attempt to accommodate requests for a specific day; however, attorneys, self-represented litigants, parties, and witnesses should be prepared to be available during the entire trial week.

Other Division Procedures
ADA Accommodations:
Under the
Americans with Disabilities Act
, if you are a person with a disability who needs any accommodation in order to participate in a proceeding, you are entitled to be provided with certain assistance at no cost to you. Please contact the ADA Coordinator, Alachua County Family and Civil Justice Center, 201 East University Avenue, Gainesville, FL 32601 (
352-337-6237
) at least 7 days before your scheduled court appearance, or immediately upon receiving this notification if the time before the scheduled appearance is less than 7 days. If you are hearing or voice-impaired, call
1-800-955-8770
via Florida Relay Service.
Interpreter Requests:
All requests should be made in advance whenever possible and in writing to Court Administration via e-mail at:
interpreter@circuit8.org
.  If you do not receive a confirmation request within two business days prior to the hearing or event, please contact the Court Interpreter at (352) 264-7063. If the Court Interpreter is unavailable, contact Court Administration at (352) 264-7029.
Please be sure to include the following information when making your request:
Date, time and location of the event
Language desired, include any geographical preferences (ex. Brazilian Portuguese)
The case number and name of the person(s) requiring interpretation
The name and telephone of the person making the request
The type of event and any special needs or instructions
AI IN THE COURTS
If any attorney for a party, or a pro se party, has used Artificial Intelligence (“AI”) in the preparation of any complaint, answer, motion, brief, or other paper filed with the Court, the attorney or pro se party MUST, in a clear and plain factual statement disclose that AI has been used in any way in the filing, and CERTIFY, that each and every citation to the law or the record in the paper has been verified as accurate.
